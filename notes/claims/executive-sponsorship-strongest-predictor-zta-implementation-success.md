# academic.2: Executive sponsorship is the strongest predictor of ZTA implementation success (β = 0.342), and phased deployment significantly outperforms big-bang approaches — both findings converge with practitioner literature despite synthetic data limitations.

**Source:** [[Academic — ZT Research Papers]] — Various, *Academic ZT Research Papers*, 2018-2024

## The Claim

Multiple regression (R² = 0.847) identifies executive sponsorship as the most significant factor (β = 0.342, p < 0.001), followed by dedicated implementation team (β = 0.251) and phased deployment (β = 0.187). Industry adoption patterns: financial services leads (28%, 18-month timeline, 77% incident response cost reduction); technology follows (26%, 14 months, but 43% required significant mid-deployment design changes); healthcare is slower but most stable (18%, 24 months, **94% require no post-go-live modifications**).

## Evidence

Medium enterprises (1,000–5,000 employees) showed optimal implementation efficiency — scale ≠ advantage. Healthcare's 94% no-post-go-live-modifications rate is the standout finding, suggesting the sector's regulatory-driven, methodical approach produces more durable implementations. The 43% mid-deployment redesign rate in technology is a cautionary counterpoint to the books' linear implementation narratives.

## Confidence

**Rating:** MEDIUM
**Rationale:** MEDIUM — The directional findings converge strongly with the entire ZT practitioner corpus (NIST, CISA, Garbis & Chapman, Green-Ortiz all emphasize executive sponsorship and phased approach). However, the specific β coefficients and the 94% healthcare stability figure derive from synthetic data and should be treated as directional hypotheses, not measured facts.
