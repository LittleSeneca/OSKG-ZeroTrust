# OSKG-ZeroTrust

> *"Never trust, always verify." — John Kindervag, 2010*
>
> Or maybe: *"Zero Trust is not a product. It's a strategy." — Chase Cunningham*

This is the home note for **OSKG-ZeroTrust** — the Open Source Knowledge Graph on Zero Trust architecture and the transformation of enterprise security. The project applies the same structured evidence synthesis methodology as [[../OSKG-YahWeh/Home|OSKG-YahWeh]] to the domain of Zero Trust: decomposing foundational texts into discrete claims with typed edges, building a traversable argument graph, and synthesizing what the evidence actually shows.

## Structure

- **[[Sources Index]]** — Primary materials: books, papers, standards, government publications
- **[[Notes Index]]** — Analysis, synthesis, and structured claims
- **[[Canvases Index|Canvases]]** — Visual maps of concepts and relationships
- **[[Book Guide]]** — The canonical reading list for this knowledge graph

## Approach

1. **Primary sources first.** What do the original texts actually say? Not what vendors repackage them to mean.
2. **Consensus where it exists.** Where practitioners disagree, understand why — technology differences, organizational context, or genuine conceptual disputes.
3. **Steelman every position.** If I can't articulate the strongest version of a Zero Trust approach I reject, I don't understand it yet.
4. **Follow the architecture.** Zero Trust is an architectural paradigm, not a product category. Evaluate claims against architectural principles, not market positioning.
